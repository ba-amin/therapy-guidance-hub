## 3 Core emotional needs and healthy alternatives

### 3.1 Which core emotional needs may be unmet or frustrated?

Name Young et al.'s **five broad needs groupings**. “Hurt” here means insufficient support or frustration, not a damaged person. The following is a conceptual formulation to explore, not an empirically established one-to-one mapping. A current unmet need does not prove a particular childhood history. [1](#ref-1)

All situations and dialogue below are **original fictional drafting alternatives**. The rendered guide uses Darya's drawing, Omid's request for a pause, Mina's acceptance concerns, and Reza's personally chosen interests to illustrate the same four needs. These outline alternatives may inform later revisions; they are not additional cases that readers must complete. Preserve equivalent meaning in Persian, using the named framework rather than treating every useful activity as a separate formal category.

| Need within the framework | Possible relevance | Fictional frustration | Possible support |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spontaneity and play — central | Enjoyment can become contingent on endlessly improving performance. | Mina turns an enjoyable sketch into a graded project: “A hobby must produce excellent work.” | Set aside a short, optional period for experimenting with color without displaying or evaluating the result. Rest/enjoyment are applications here, not a sixth formal need. |
| Freedom to express valid needs and emotions — central | Pressure can silence fatigue, uncertainty, frustration, or requests for support. | Sara feels hungry but thinks asking for a break would show weakness. | Name hunger and the workload; take an ordinary lunch break and clarify the actual scheduling requirement. |
| Secure attachment, including acceptance — context-dependent | A person may connect being accepted with exceptional performance. | After an ordinary error, Nima expects warmth to disappear and conceals his disappointment. | A supportive person acknowledges disappointment, remains caring, and helps correct the specific error without humiliation. Do not infer this fear in every reader. |
| Autonomy, competence, and identity — context-dependent | Personal interests can become hard to distinguish from compulsory achievement goals. | Roya chooses every activity by what will look impressive, while unsure what she enjoys. | Explore one personally meaningful interest and allow novice-level learning; retain choice about achievement. |

**Complete fictional need/support example:** Mina has time for a short sketch. A minor uneven line leads to “If it is not excellent, this is wasted time,” pressure, and repeated erasing. The immediate need is room for play. She keeps a deliberately private practice page and experiments for a few minutes. The result remains uneven and the urge to judge it remains; she notices whether there was any enjoyment. This is an illustration of support for play, not a validated exercise or proof of an origin.

### 3.2 A healthier, more flexible alternative

Retain a comparison of rigid demands and flexible ambition across purpose, stopping points, response to mistakes, satisfaction, relationships, and costs. A realistic standard can include correction and commitment while leaving room for limits and other needs. Do not reinterpret “realistic limits and self-control” as a reason to impose still stricter rules on this reader.

## 4 Recognizing the pattern

### 4.1 Visible signs / نشانه‌های آشکار

Use possible observations such as revising after agreed requirements are met, repeatedly correcting another person's harmless preferences, difficulty delegating, postponing a beginner task, and extending work into planned breaks. Separate actions from guessed motives.

**Original fictional example:** A colleague sees Sara rewrite a short scheduling reply several times. Sara reports “The wording must be perfect,” worry, and tight shoulders. The observable rewriting does not by itself reveal her meaning; checking necessary information could have another explanation.

### 4.2 Hidden or masked signs / نشانه‌های پنهان یا پوشیده

**Original fictional example:** Farid appears relaxed and says a course is uninteresting. Privately he wants to join but expects his first assignment to be inadequate. Avoiding enrolment briefly spares him that pressure and may cost him a valued learning opportunity. This is one possible avoidance function; real time, money, access, or interest constraints are alternatives. Link to section 7.

Other possible private experiences include pressure concealed by achievement, feeling unable to enjoy praise, and guilt while resting. These are reflection aids, not validated sign categories. A competent person can work hard and still stop flexibly; a person may decline a course simply because it does not fit. Readers can conclude the schema does not fit them; “hidden” never makes disagreement evidence for it.

### 4.3 Common triggers and early cues / محرک‌های رایج و نشانه‌های اولیه

Distinguish events (a deadline, a minor correction, somebody else's different method, unstructured free time) from meanings (“I must do more”). Invite recognition of urgency, irritability, or shoulder/jaw tension without presenting bodily cues as specific symptoms. Check genuine workload, caregiving, disability, practical capacity, cultural expectations, financial pressure, and actual requirements before assigning a schema explanation.

## 5 Possible origins and unmet needs

Explore possible experiences of conditional praise, criticism, modeled overwork, or little permission for play and emotional expression. [1](#ref-1) [5](#ref-5) School, peers, work, temperament, and present pressures may also matter. Keep every origin tentative and connect it to section 3 without repeating the needs table. High expectations do not prove abusive or unloving caregiving; neither recognition prompts nor memory gaps establish a cause. Do not require recalling a painful event.

| Reinforcement | “Next time I must work faster.” She attributes getting through the task to extra effort and carries a stricter efficiency demand into the next message. | «دفعهٔ بعد باید سریع‌تر کار کنم.» پایان‌دادن کار را نتیجهٔ تلاش اضافی می‌داند و توقع کاراییِ سخت‌گیرانه‌تری را به پیام بعدی می‌برد. |

**Healthy Adult interruption — EN:** At the first urge to rewrite again, Sara says, “The availability details need to be clear; the wording does not need to be perfect. Lunch matters too.” She checks those details once and sends the adequate reply. The colleague asks one clarification; she answers it. Worry remains, and lunch becomes possible. This mixed result offers learning about proportionate effort and ordinary repair, not a guarantee of flawless outcomes.

**مداخلهٔ بزرگسال سالم — FA:** با نخستین میل به بازنویسی دوباره، سارا می‌گوید: «اطلاعات زمان‌های آزاد باید روشن باشد؛ لازم نیست جمله‌بندی بی‌نقص باشد. ناهار هم مهم است.» اطلاعات را یک بار بررسی و پاسخ کافی را ارسال می‌کند. همکارش یک سؤال برای روشن‌شدن موضوع می‌پرسد و سارا پاسخ می‌دهد. نگرانی باقی می‌ماند، اما فرصتی برای ناهار فراهم می‌شود. این نتیجهٔ ترکیبی فرصتی برای یادگیری دربارهٔ تلاش متناسب و اصلاح معمولی است، نه تضمین نتیجهٔ بی‌نقص.

## 7 Coping styles and modes

### 7.1 How the concepts relate

Keep these distinctions visible before optional examples:

- **Schema / طرحواره:** the recurring meaning or rule; here, rigidly demanding exceptional performance.
- **Coping style / سبک مقابله‌ای:** how a person responds to that pattern—**surrender / تسلیم**, **avoidance / اجتناب**, or **overcompensation / جبران افراطی**. A coping response is the particular action in one situation.
- **Mode / ذهنیت:** the temporary configuration of feelings, messages, and responses active now. Modes can change during one encounter. They are not identities, diagnoses, or a prescribed list for everyone. [1](#ref-1)

### 7.2 Three ways of coping with this schema

All rows are **original fictional illustrations / مثال‌های فرضی تألیفی**. Ask what the action accomplishes before choosing a label. Ordinary persistence, rest, refusal, or carelessness alone does not establish a coping style.

| Possible style | Trigger and internal meaning | Action and immediate function | Possible later cost or repetition | Flexible alternative |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Surrender / تسلیم** | A correct report contains uneven spacing. “If it can be improved, I am obliged to keep working.” | Reformatting long after the task meets its purpose; obeying the demanding rule briefly eases pressure. | Less sleep, an expanding definition of “finished,” and the belief that relentless work was necessary. | Check the agreed purpose and essential requirements, then stop at a reasonable point while allowing some unease: “Further polishing is optional here; sleep also matters.” |
| **Avoidance / اجتناب** | An interesting course requires submitting ordinary practice work. “My first attempt will not meet my standard.” | Avoiding enrolment or never opening the assignment to escape the expected pressure and distress. | Lost learning opportunities; the person never discovers what an adequate first attempt could accomplish. First check real time, money, access, or skill barriers. | If the course fits available resources and interests, clarify what a first attempt requires and try one small practice task with support: “A learning exercise can be unfinished and still teach me something.” |
| **Overcompensation / جبران افراطی** | A request for a reasonable task format activates “I must meet every demand perfectly.” | Swinging into rigid defiance—“No standard applies to me”—and deliberately disregarding even useful agreements, trying to reverse the felt control of standards. | Conflict and preventable problems may fuel a return to harsher demands. | Separate useful requirements from unnecessary demands and negotiate the latter: “I can choose a reasonable agreement without accepting every expectation.” A thoughtful boundary or lowering an unnecessary standard is a healthy alternative, not this pattern. |

The same person may use different styles. Excessive work that obeys this schema is **surrender**; high achievement used to disprove defectiveness may **overcompensate for Defectiveness/Shame**. State which schema is being formulated rather than calling all perfectionistic effort overcompensation. [1](#ref-1)

### 7.3 Modes that may become active

Demanding Parent concerns rigid expectations; Punitive Parent concerns blame and punishment. Vulnerable Child describes a distressed state; coping modes describe attempts to manage distress. Healthy Adult combines care, realistic responsibility, and limits. “Parent” is a model term for an internal message, not proof of who caused it. [4](#ref-4)

Use the following **original recognition illustrations**, not a checklist that assigns modes. A reader may recognize only some rows, use an ordinary-language description, or be unsure. All inner speech below is invented, not quoted from a source.

| Possible mode (EN / FA) | Activation cue and inner speech | Feelings, body, and visible response | Need to notice and possible Healthy Adult response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Demanding Parent / والد پرتوقع** | A task reaches an adequate stopping point: “Enough is still below what I should do.” | Pressure, irritability, tight jaw; adding unnecessary tasks or criticizing small imperfections. | Realistic limits and permission for rest: “What is actually required here, and when will I stop?” |
| **Punitive Parent / والد تنبیه‌گر** | An error or an unfinished item: “You do not deserve a pleasant evening after that.” | Self-directed anger, shame, bodily tension; insults or withholding ordinary comfort as punishment. | Accountability without cruelty: correct the specific error, retain food, rest, and connection, and refuse global condemnation. |
| **Vulnerable Child / کودک آسیب‌پذیر**, when reported | Imagining a disappointed colleague: “I feel frightened and alone with this pressure.” | Fear or sadness, sinking feeling; seeking reassurance or wanting to hide. | Reassurance, acceptance, and support: “This is difficult; you can ask for help and still be treated with respect.” Do not require this state or assume shame beneath every standard. |
| **Compliant Surrenderer / تسلیم‌شوندهٔ مطیع**, where interpersonal submission fits | Someone asks for repeated unnecessary revisions: “I cannot say I have reached my limit; they may reject me.” | Apprehension, small voice, tense posture; agreeing while concealing a need for time or rest. | Voice, boundaries, and realistic negotiation. Explore Approval-Seeking or Subjugation as well. Following one's own demanding rule alone does not establish this interpersonal mode. |
| **Detached Protector / محافظ جداشده**, where emotional disconnection fits | Pressure becomes overwhelming: “I want to feel nothing about any of this.” | Numbness or disconnection; compulsive distraction and withdrawal from supportive contact. | A tolerable pause followed by reconnection: notice one feeling, choose restorative rest, and return to one manageable decision. Enjoying a chosen break is not evidence of detachment. |
| **Overcompensator / جبران‌گر افراطی**, where an excessive counterreaction fits | A reasonable request feels like being controlled: “I will show that nobody can expect anything of me.” | Defiant anger, rigid posture; dismissing every agreement, even ones the person values. | Autonomy with reciprocal responsibility: separate an excessive demand from a useful agreement, then negotiate. Do not label assertiveness or independence as pathological. |
| **Healthy Adult / بزرگسال سالم** | Noticing “must,” self-insult, shutdown, or defiance: “What does this situation need, and what do I need?” | Concern can remain; a slower breath and more room to choose may appear. Clarifies purpose, repairs an error, asks for help, or stops proportionately. | Care, competence, rest, connection, and flexible limits. This mode need not feel perfectly calm or make the discomfort disappear. |

**Alternative fictional branch — EN:** Faced with the same routine scheduling request, Sara leaves the message unopened so she does not encounter the pressure to compose a perfect answer. Not opening it briefly distances her from the discomfort, but the scheduling decision remains unresolved. She could open it when she has time, check what information is needed, and send a proportionate answer with support if helpful.

**شاخهٔ فرضی دیگر — FA:** سارا در برابر همان درخواست معمول برای هماهنگی زمان، پیام را باز نمی‌کند تا با فشارِ نوشتن پاسخی بی‌نقص روبه‌رو نشود. بازنکردن پیام برای مدتی او را از ناراحتی دور می‌کند، اما تصمیم دربارهٔ زمان همچنان حل‌نشده می‌ماند. این واکنش ممکن است کارکرد اجتنابی داشته باشد؛ ناخوانده‌ماندن پیام به‌تنهایی دلیل آن را مشخص نمی‌کند. سارا می‌تواند وقتی فرصت دارد پیام را باز کند، اطلاعات لازم را بررسی کند و در صورت نیاز با کمک دیگران پاسخی متناسب بفرستد.

## 8 Everyday examples

Retain fictional work, study, parenting, relationship, household, and rest examples. In a partner example, show how unsolicited correction may reduce connection, how the recipient can express its effect, and how a specific apology and agreed division of responsibility support repair. Another person's patience is not a guaranteed outcome or their obligation. Include a demand, cost, flexible alternative, and uncertainty. In each, identify a specific activation cue, one possible mode or ordinary-language state, and the coping function. Avoid copying an identical mode sequence into every context.

**Implemented fictional worked example / مثال فرضی کامل:** Neda's friend has arrived and the kitchen is clean and usable. Two cups on the counter prompt “Until everything is impeccable, I have no right to sit down,” with tight shoulders and possible Demanding Parent pressure. Cleaning to obey the rule briefly eases pressure but leaves her friend waiting. Regret may shift into punitive self-attack. A Healthy Adult response recognizes connection, enjoyment, and limits: she apologizes for the wait and sits down, although tension and her friend's disappointment remain. Later she considers what helped her stay engaged. A genuine hygiene or slip hazard would require a different response; enjoying order does not establish a schema.

## 9 Related patterns

Use the same event—editing an accurate report again—to compare approval-seeking, defectiveness/shame, failure, and punitiveness. For each show what looks similar, what meaning differs, and that both patterns may be relevant. [3](#ref-3)

| Related pattern | Possible original meaning in the same situation | Distinction from an internal demand for exceptional performance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Approval-Seeking / Recognition-Seeking | “What matters is whether the colleague admires me.” | Approval or recognition is central; a demanding standard can remain even without an audience. |
| Defectiveness / Shame | “This will expose something unacceptable about me.” | Global unworthiness differs from the rule about how high performance must be; they may interact. |
| Failure | “I cannot perform as capably as other people.” | Expected incompetence in achievement differs from requiring exceptional performance. |
| Punitiveness | “A mistake means I deserve punishment.” | Punishment for error differs from the standard itself. Sara's later message illustrates a possible shift. |

These are fictional comparisons, not a way to diagnose from a sentence. Link to existing relevant guides. Distinguish conceptual overlap from empirical association; recognizing any pattern does not establish a psychiatric diagnosis.

## 10 Examining your experience

Keep recognition prompts original, optional, and unscored. For example: “What standard felt compulsory?” “Who actually required it?” “What did extra effort improve or displace?” “Which need was overlooked?” Readers may skip, write “not sure,” or use ordinary descriptions rather than mode names.

## 11 Practicing change

Use original, manageable practices: define a sufficient stopping point for a routine communication; allow a small enjoyable activity without evaluating achievement; or express one realistic limit respectfully. CCI is supplementary CBT guidance, not validation of these exercises. [9](#ref-9) Never relax actual safety, medical, legal, or caregiving requirements as an experiment. If consequences could be serious, clarify the requirement with the responsible person first.

Keep the same six shared planner fields. Integrate the activation cue, anticipated demanding/punitive message, Healthy Adult sentence and action, and optional support within those fields.

**Original completed experiment — Sara**

| Standard field | Completed fictional example |
| --- | --- |
| Situation and limits | A routine meeting-scheduling reply; retain accurate availability and any real deadline. |
| Prediction | If I stop after one check of necessary details, the colleague will complain that I am careless. I expect tight shoulders, “not enough,” and an urge to rewrite. |
| Small action | At tight shoulders and the urge to rewrite, check necessary details once, say “Clear information is enough here,” and send. |
| Support or stopping conditions | If the request has consequential or unclear requirements, clarify them first. If pressure becomes overwhelming, pause and seek support rather than force the experiment. |
| Observed outcome | One clarification was requested and answered; the predicted complaint was not observed in this exchange. Worry remained; lunch was possible. |
| Learning and next adjustment | A question can be ordinary coordination rather than proof of failure. Next time name available times clearly, without adding a new perfection rule. |

## 12 How therapy can help

Outline collaborative assessment of present difficulties, meanings, needs, context, and goals. Explain cognitive work on rigid rules, behavioral practice, relational patterns and repair, and therapist-supported experiential work. [1](#ref-1) [5](#ref-5) Distinguish a full treatment from this educational guide and discuss preferences and pace.

### When professional support may help / چه زمانی کمک حرفه‌ای می‌تواند مفید باشد؟

Give visible guidance: consider support when pressure repeatedly interferes with sleep, work, relationships, rest, or health; when self-criticism feels difficult to interrupt; or when understanding the pattern alone is not enough. The reader can bring one recent example and a practical goal. Intensive imagery, chair work, and painful-memory work require therapeutic support rather than instructions for independent practice.

## 13 Reviewing progress

Review greater choice, clearer stopping points, receiving support, repair after criticism, and recovery after setbacks. These are original progress ideas, not treatment criteria or promises. Include realistic versus unrealistic expectations explicitly:

| Realistic direction | Unhelpful recovery demand |
| --- | --- |
| Notice pressure sooner, sometimes after acting. | Catch every mode before it appears. |
| Act proportionately while worry remains. | Never feel anxious about imperfect work. |
| Correct a specific error and reconnect after criticism. | Never make a mistake or affect a relationship. |
| Return to a useful practice after setbacks. | Finish every worksheet and improve continuously. |

Invite noticing a shift from demand to punishment or withdrawal, and whether a later caring response restored choice. Do not count modes or turn Healthy Adult into a perfect identity. **Original prompt:** “Which early sign did I notice, and where did I gain a little more choice?” / «کدام نشانهٔ اولیه را متوجه شدم و کجا کمی امکان انتخاب بیشتری پیدا کردم؟»

## 14 What research supports

Separate conceptual accounts [1, 3–5], schema-specific observational associations [7], a whole-treatment trial [6], a multicomponent digital/perfectionism trial [8], and supplementary CBT guidance [9]. The sources do not validate this guide or its original exercises.

Use the concise findings and limitations in the [evidence record](../research/unrelenting-standards-sources.md). Koerner's N = 138 probable-GAD study is associational and abstract-only; retain “status or severity.” Bamelis's N = 323 trial concerns a full treatment for personality disorders, not this schema alone; retain the lack of between-treatment differences on self-report measures. Jeong's analyzed perfectionism subgroup is 54 (34 intervention, 20 waitlist); totals of 300 randomized/218 analyzed cover four modules. Its perfectionism measure was HMPS, not the YSQ; preserve completion-only, waitlist, and brief follow-up limitations. Do not imply direct schema change.
