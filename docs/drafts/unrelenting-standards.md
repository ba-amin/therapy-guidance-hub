# Unrelenting Standards / Hypercriticalness guide draft

Authoring outline for **معیارهای سرسختانه / عیب‌جویی افراطی**. Follow the [shared standard](../schema-guide-standard.md) and [reusable template](../schema-guide-template.md). The current guide largely follows this sequence: align labels and repeated structures while retaining verified material. This editorial draft includes original illustrations and exercises, not a clinical assessment.

## 1 Getting started

Offer the shared recognition, examination, and practice paths. Readers can stop after one useful observation. Original opening:

**EN:** “When finishing one task only reveals the next demand, it can help to examine the rules behind the pressure.”

**FA:** «وقتی تمام‌کردن یک کار فقط توقع بعدی را آشکار می‌کند، بررسی قواعد پشت این فشار می‌تواند کمک‌کننده باشد.»

## 2 Understanding this schema

Define damaging pressure to meet exceptionally high internal standards. Introduce perfectionism, rigid rules, and preoccupation with time and efficiency. [1](#ref-1)

Original core-rule example: **“I must keep improving; stopping means I am falling behind.”** / **«باید مدام بهتر شوم؛ توقف یعنی عقب‌افتادن.»** Explain that ambition alone does not establish this pattern.

## 3 Healthy needs and flexible alternatives

Use the shared comparison table: motivation, flexibility, mistakes, satisfaction, and costs. Illustrate demanding standards and flexible alternatives. Include competence alongside rest, enjoyment, connection, and realistic limits. Avoid implying that responsibility or achievement must be abandoned.

## 4 Recognizing the pattern

Organize optional observations by thoughts, emotions, body, actions, and triggers. Examples: unnecessary editing, irritation during interruptions, tension while resting, and postponing tasks whose ideal version feels unreachable. Consider workload and circumstances before attributing everything to a schema.

## 5 Possible origins and unmet needs

Explore remembered expectations about performance, mistakes, leisure, and usefulness across school, peers, family, and work. Ask what permission or support was missing. Keep origins tentative; high expectations do not establish abusive or unloving caregiving.

## 6 How the cycle continues

Place this **Fictional example / مثال فرضی** in the shared six-step expandable cycle. Mode names are tentative descriptions of this person's experience; they are not stages everyone must pass through. Introduce the names in section 7 and make that section easy to reach from the cycle.

**Complete sequence — EN**

1. **Trigger:** Leila has finished a routine presentation and notices a minor spacing difference before going home. The agreed requirements are already met.
2. **Meaning and activation:** “Every slide must be flawless” is her **Demanding Parent / والد پرتوقع** message. Tight shoulders and urgency follow. When she imagines disappointing people, she feels small and frightened: a possible **Vulnerable Child / کودک آسیب‌پذیر** state. That vulnerable state is included because Leila reports it, not inferred from her careful work.
3. **Coping response:** She edits for another hour, surrendering to the rule that adequate work is unacceptable. After finding another tiny inconsistency, her message shifts to “You deserve to lose your evening for missing that”: possible **Punitive Parent / والد تنبیه‌گر**. Overwhelmed, she then scrolls without noticing her feelings or her partner's greeting: possible **Detached Protector / محافظ جداشده**, functioning as avoidance. This is a change in coping function, not simply more perfectionism.
4. **Immediate effects:** Editing briefly reduces her sense of being at fault; scrolling briefly blunts the pressure. Neither response tests whether a sufficient presentation would have served its purpose.
5. **Later costs:** She loses sleep and connection. The next day she is tired and struggles to concentrate. The punitive message treats tiredness as another personal failure, restarting demands for extra work.
6. **Reinforcement:** “I only managed because I pushed so hard; I must be stricter next time.” The relief from editing and detachment makes both responses tempting at the next deadline. Actual feedback about the presentation's usefulness is overlooked.

**Healthy Adult interruption / مداخلهٔ بزرگسال سالم:** At the first cue, Leila checks the agreed purpose, acknowledges her worry, and says, “One formatting difference does not require punishment. The information is correct; I can stop and reconnect.” If she has already withdrawn, she can return, explain that she felt overwhelmed, and take a genuine break. Her anxiety may remain, and feedback may still identify an improvement. The learning concerns proportionate effort and recovery, not proof that mistakes never matter.

**توالی کامل — FA**

1. **محرک:** لیلا آماده‌کردن یک ارائهٔ معمولی را تمام کرده و پیش از رفتن به خانه متوجه تفاوت کوچکی در فاصله‌گذاری می‌شود. الزامات توافق‌شده از قبل برآورده شده‌اند.
2. **معنا و فعال‌شدن حالت:** «همهٔ اسلایدها باید بی‌نقص باشند» پیام **والد پرتوقع** اوست. شانه‌هایش منقبض می‌شوند و احساس فوریت می‌کند. وقتی تصور می‌کند دیگران را ناامید کرده است، احساس کوچکی و ترس به سراغش می‌آید: حالتی که شاید **کودک آسیب‌پذیر** توصیف مناسبی برای آن باشد. این حالت به این دلیل در مثال آمده که لیلا آن را گزارش می‌کند؛ از دقت او در کار نتیجه گرفته نشده است.
3. **پاسخ مقابله‌ای:** یک ساعت دیگر ویرایش می‌کند و تسلیم این قاعده می‌شود که کارِ کافی پذیرفتنی نیست. با پیداکردن ناهماهنگی کوچک دیگری، پیامش تغییر می‌کند: «چون متوجه آن نشده بودی، سزاوار این هستی که عصرِ استراحتت را از دست بدهی»؛ شاید **والد تنبیه‌گر** فعال شده باشد. سپس، زیر فشار احساس‌ها، بدون توجه به احساس خودش یا سلام شریک زندگی‌اش به مرور مطالب می‌پردازد؛ شاید **محافظ جداشده** فعال باشد و پاسخ، کارکرد اجتنابی پیدا کرده باشد. این تغییرِ کارکرد مقابله است، نه فقط کمال‌گرایی بیشتر.
4. **پیامدهای فوری:** ویرایش برای مدتی احساس مقصربودن او را کم می‌کند و مرور مطالب نیز موقتاً فشار را کاهش می‌دهد. هیچ‌کدام فرصتی نمی‌دهند تا ببیند آیا ارائه‌ای که به‌اندازهٔ کافی آماده شده بود، هدفش را برآورده می‌کرد.
5. **هزینه‌های بعدی:** از خواب و ارتباطش با دیگران کم می‌شود. روز بعد خسته است و تمرکزکردن برایش دشوار می‌شود. پیام تنبیه‌گر خستگی را شکست شخصی دیگری می‌داند و دوباره توقعِ کار بیشتر را فعال می‌کند.
6. **تقویت الگو:** «فقط چون خیلی به خودم فشار آوردم از پسش برآمدم؛ دفعهٔ بعد باید سخت‌گیرتر باشم.» آسودگی ناشی از ویرایش و فاصله‌گرفتن از احساس‌ها، هر دو پاسخ را در مهلت بعدی وسوسه‌انگیز می‌کند. بازخورد واقعی دربارهٔ مفیدبودن ارائه نادیده می‌ماند.

**مداخلهٔ بزرگسال سالم — FA:** لیلا با نخستین نشانه، هدف توافق‌شده را بررسی می‌کند، نگرانی‌اش را به رسمیت می‌شناسد و می‌گوید: «یک تفاوت در قالب‌بندی نیاز به تنبیه ندارد. اطلاعات درست است؛ می‌توانم توقف کنم و دوباره با دیگران ارتباط بگیرم.» اگر از قبل کناره گرفته باشد، می‌تواند برگردد، توضیح بدهد که احساس فشار زیادی داشته و استراحتی واقعی کند. ممکن است اضطرابش باقی بماند و بازخورد دیگران همچنان نکته‌ای برای بهبود نشان بدهد. موضوعِ یادگیری، تلاش متناسب و بازیابی توان است، نه اثبات اینکه اشتباه‌ها هرگز اهمیت ندارند.

Keep all six steps understandable without opening every disclosure. Add an optional prompt: “Where did the pressure, feeling, or coping purpose change?” / «فشار، احساس یا هدف مقابله در کدام نقطه تغییر کرد؟»

## 7 Coping styles and modes

Make this a required teaching section, with the distinctions visible before optional examples:

- **Schema / طرحواره:** the recurring meaning or rule; here, rigidly demanding exceptional performance.
- **Coping style / سبک مقابله‌ای:** how a person responds to that pattern—**surrender / تسلیم**, **avoidance / اجتناب**, or **overcompensation / جبران افراطی**. A coping response is the particular action in one situation.
- **Mode / ذهنیت:** the temporary configuration of feelings, messages, and responses active now. Modes can change during one encounter. They are not identities, diagnoses, or a prescribed list for everyone. [2](#ref-2)

### Three coping styles in this schema

All rows are **original fictional illustrations / مثال‌های فرضی تألیفی**. Ask what the action accomplishes before choosing a label. Ordinary persistence, rest, refusal, or carelessness alone does not establish a coping style.

| Possible style | Trigger and internal meaning | Action and immediate function | Possible later cost or repetition | Flexible alternative |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Surrender / تسلیم** | A correct report contains uneven spacing. “If it can be improved, I am obliged to keep working.” | Reformatting long after the task meets its purpose; obeying the demanding rule briefly eases pressure. | Less sleep, an expanding definition of “finished,” and the belief that relentless work was necessary. | Check the agreed purpose and essential requirements, then stop at a reasonable point while allowing some unease: “Further polishing is optional here; sleep also matters.” |
| **Avoidance / اجتناب** | An interesting course requires submitting ordinary practice work. “My first attempt will not meet my standard.” | Avoiding enrolment or never opening the assignment to escape the expected pressure and distress. | Lost learning opportunities; the person never discovers what an adequate first attempt could accomplish. First check real time, money, access, or skill barriers. | If the course fits available resources and interests, clarify what a first attempt requires and try one small practice task with support: “A learning exercise can be unfinished and still teach me something.” |
| **Overcompensation / جبران افراطی** | A request for a reasonable task format activates “I must meet every demand perfectly.” | Swinging into rigid defiance—“No standard applies to me”—and deliberately disregarding even useful agreements, trying to reverse the felt control of standards. | Conflict and preventable problems may fuel a return to harsher demands. | Separate useful requirements from unnecessary demands and negotiate the latter: “I can choose a reasonable agreement without accepting every expectation.” A thoughtful boundary or lowering an unnecessary standard is a healthy alternative, not this pattern. |

The same person may use different styles. Excessive work that obeys this schema is **surrender**; high achievement used to disprove defectiveness may **overcompensate for Defectiveness/Shame**. State which schema is being formulated rather than calling all perfectionistic effort overcompensation. [2](#ref-2)

### Recognizing possible modes as they become active

Demanding Parent concerns rigid expectations; Punitive Parent concerns blame and punishment. Vulnerable Child describes a distressed state; coping modes describe attempts to manage distress. Healthy Adult combines care, realistic responsibility, and limits. “Parent” is a model term for an internal message, not proof of who caused it. [3](#ref-3)

Use the following **original recognition illustrations**, not a checklist that assigns modes. A reader may recognize only some rows, use an ordinary-language description, or be unsure. All inner speech below is invented, not quoted from a source.

| Possible mode (EN / FA) | Activation cue and inner speech | Feelings, body, and visible response | Need to notice and possible Healthy Adult response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Demanding Parent / والد پرتوقع** | A task reaches an adequate stopping point: “Enough is still below what I should do.” | Pressure, irritability, tight jaw; adding unnecessary tasks or criticizing small imperfections. | Realistic limits and permission for rest: “What is actually required here, and when will I stop?” |
| **Punitive Parent / والد تنبیه‌گر** | An error or an unfinished item: “You do not deserve a pleasant evening after that.” | Self-directed anger, shame, bodily tension; insults or withholding ordinary comfort as punishment. | Accountability without cruelty: correct the specific error, retain food, rest, and connection, and refuse global condemnation. |
| **Vulnerable Child / کودک آسیب‌پذیر**, when reported | Imagining a disappointed colleague: “I feel frightened and alone with this pressure.” | Fear or sadness, sinking feeling; seeking reassurance or wanting to hide. | Reassurance, acceptance, and support: “This is difficult; you can ask for help and still be treated with respect.” Do not require this state or assume shame beneath every standard. |
| **Compliant Surrenderer / تسلیم‌شوندهٔ مطیع**, where interpersonal submission fits | Someone asks for repeated unnecessary revisions: “I cannot say I have reached my limit; they may reject me.” | Apprehension, small voice, tense posture; agreeing while concealing a need for time or rest. | Voice, boundaries, and realistic negotiation. Explore Approval-Seeking or Subjugation as well. Following one's own demanding rule alone does not establish this interpersonal mode. |
| **Detached Protector / محافظ جداشده**, where emotional disconnection fits | Pressure becomes overwhelming: “I want to feel nothing about any of this.” | Numbness or disconnection; compulsive distraction and withdrawal from supportive contact. | A tolerable pause followed by reconnection: notice one feeling, choose restorative rest, and return to one manageable decision. Enjoying a chosen break is not evidence of detachment. |
| **Overcompensator / جبران‌گر افراطی**, where an excessive counterreaction fits | A reasonable request feels like being controlled: “I will show that nobody can expect anything of me.” | Defiant anger, rigid posture; dismissing every agreement, even ones the person values. | Autonomy with reciprocal responsibility: separate an excessive demand from a useful agreement, then negotiate. Do not label assertiveness or independence as pathological. |
| **Healthy Adult / بزرگسال سالم** | Noticing “must,” self-insult, shutdown, or defiance: “What does this situation need, and what do I need?” | Concern can remain; a slower breath and more room to choose may appear. Clarifies purpose, repairs an error, asks for help, or stops proportionately. | Care, competence, rest, connection, and flexible limits. This mode need not feel perfectly calm or make the discomfort disappear. |

The temporary modes above are possibilities, not a fixed mapping of Unrelenting Standards to seven modes. Link to [section 6's complete bilingual sequence](#6-how-the-cycle-continues) to show **activation, switches, coping function, relief, costs, reinforcement, and interruption** together without repeating the whole sequence here. Keep the same labels and qualifications in both languages.

**Alternative fictional branch, same trigger:** On another occasion, the same small spacing difference leads Leila to close the presentation without sending it, hoping to escape the pressure of anyone seeing imperfect work: avoidance rather than surrender through extra editing. The brief relief leaves the deadline unresolved; a Healthy Adult response checks the agreed requirements and sends the adequate version with appropriate support, even if discomfort remains.

**شاخهٔ فرضی دیگر با همان محرک:** بار دیگر، همان تفاوت کوچک در فاصله‌گذاری باعث می‌شود لیلا فایل را بدون ارسال ببندد تا از فشار دیده‌شدنِ کاری که بی‌نقص نیست دور بماند؛ این واکنش اجتناب است، نه تسلیم از راه ویرایش بیشتر. آسودگی کوتاه، مسئلهٔ مهلت را حل نمی‌کند؛ پاسخ بزرگسال سالم بررسی معیارهای توافق‌شده و ارسال نسخهٔ کافی با حمایت مناسب است، حتی اگر ناراحتی باقی بماند.

## 8 Everyday examples

Retain fictional work, study, parenting, relationship, household, and rest examples. Include a demand, cost, flexible alternative, and uncertainty. In each, identify a specific activation cue, one possible mode or ordinary-language state, and the coping function. Avoid copying an identical mode sequence into every context.

**Fictional worked example / مثال فرضی کامل:** Amir has cleaned before friends arrive but notices an untidy cupboard. “Rest is allowed only after everything is organized” signals possible Demanding Parent pressure; he feels tension and reaches for cleaning supplies. Further cleaning would surrender to this rule and briefly ease guilt, while leaving him tired during the visit. A Healthy Adult response identifies connection and rest alongside a reasonably comfortable home. He stops when the shared rooms are comfortable, although tension remains. Later, he records whether he could participate in the visit, what remained difficult, and whether self-insults or renewed demands appeared. No mode is assigned solely because he enjoys a clean home.

## 9 Related patterns

Compare approval seeking, defectiveness/shame, failure, and punitiveness through feared consequences: disappointing oneself, losing approval, exposure, inability, or deserving punishment. Include overlap. Recognizing this schema does not establish a psychiatric diagnosis.

## 10 Examining your experience

Retain one shared eight-field worksheet input set. Original prompts: “What standard felt compulsory?” “Who actually required it?” “What did extra effort improve?” “What did it displace?” “What need was overlooked?” “What would enough mean here?” Use no scores or thresholds; permit skipping fields and switching language without losing answers.

Add optional helper text within the existing fields, without making more required inputs: field 2, “Was this a demand, a punishment message, or another meaning?”; field 3, “Which state or mode, if any, fits now; did it change?”; field 4, “Was I obeying the rule, escaping its feelings, or trying to reverse it?”; field 5, “What relief made repetition tempting?”; field 7, “What could a caring, realistic Healthy Adult response say and do?”

**EN recognition prompt:** “When the pressure rises, do I push harder, withdraw, or reject every expectation? What am I trying to prevent?”

**FA recognition prompt:** «وقتی فشار بیشتر می‌شود، بیشتر به خودم فشار می‌آورم، کنار می‌کشم یا هر انتظاری را رد می‌کنم؟ می‌خواهم از چه چیزی جلوگیری کنم؟»

Allow **“I don't know / نمی‌دانم”**, “uncertain,” and plain descriptions instead of either a mode or coping-style label. Carry both the coping function and mode changes into the completed example.

## 11 Practicing change

Use a low-consequence experiment: set a reasonable time limit for formatting a personal invitation, then send it once the essential information is correct. Record prediction, outcome, effort, and learning. Preserve actual safety and care requirements. Add optional rest practice and a cost–benefit comparison; avoid making relaxation another achievement target.

Use the existing six experiment fields to make mode work practical: identify the first activation cue, predict the demanding or punitive message, choose one Healthy Adult sentence **and** action, and plan support if avoidance or defiance appears. For example: “My shoulders tighten and I want to revise again. I will check the date and location, say ‘Clear information is sufficient for this invitation,’ and stop at the agreed point.” If punitive self-talk follows, add “I can correct an error without insulting myself.” Observe which state followed and what the action actually achieved; do not require feeling calm before acting.

**FA practice cue:** «شانه‌هایم سفت شده و می‌خواهم دوباره ویرایش کنم. تاریخ و نشانی را بررسی می‌کنم، به خودم می‌گویم «برای این دعوت‌نامه، اطلاعات روشن کافی است» و در نقطه‌ای که تعیین کرده‌ام توقف می‌کنم.»

## 12 How therapy can help

Outline collaborative assessment, examining rigid rules, behavioral change, and the therapeutic relationship. Present experiential methods as therapist-supported options; avoid requiring painful recollection or solo chair work. Connect seeking help to distress, interference, and personal preference.

## 13 Reviewing progress

Look for flexibility, earlier stopping, recovery from mistakes, and room for relationships. Invite observations, not performance metrics. Unfinished worksheets can coexist with progress. Returning to an old rule invites reflection, not a verdict of failure.

Ask whether the person notices activation sooner, distinguishes “do more” from “you deserve punishment,” recognizes a switch from pushing to disconnecting, and can meet a need without automatically following the old coping response. A later Healthy Adult response still counts: reconnecting after withdrawal or repairing an excessive reaction is useful learning. Avoid counting modes, demanding their elimination, or turning “being Healthy Adult” into another perfect standard.

**EN:** “Which early sign did I notice, and where did I gain a little more choice?”

**FA:** «کدام نشانهٔ اولیه را متوجه شدم و کجا کمی امکان انتخاب بیشتری پیدا کردم؟»
