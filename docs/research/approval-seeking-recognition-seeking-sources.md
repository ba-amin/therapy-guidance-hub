# Approval-Seeking / Recognition-Seeking: authoring evidence record

Sources reviewed **16 October 2026** for **تأییدطلبی / توجه‌طلبی**. This record accompanies the [guide](../../Interactive%20Guide%20to%20the%20Approval%20Seeking%20Recognition%20Seeking%20Schema.html) and [authoring outline](../drafts/approval-seeking-recognition-seeking.md). Reference numbers **1–9** match the guide: 1–8 are consulted sources and 9 is further reading. This is an editorial evidence record, not independent clinical validation.

All scenarios, inner sentences, bodily cues, recognition prompts, and worksheets are original educational material. They are not published cases, questionnaire items, diagnostic criteria, or tested interventions. Visible and hidden signs are teaching distinctions. Behavior alone, or disagreement with an interpretation, cannot establish a schema, mode, motive, diagnosis, or developmental history.

## 1. Young, Klosko, and Weishaar — conceptual framework

<a id="ref-1"></a>
Young, J. E., Klosko, J. S., & Weishaar, M. E. (2003). *Schema Therapy: A Practitioner's Guide*. Guilford Press. [Official Chapter 1 excerpt](https://www.guilford.com/excerpts/young.pdf?t=1).

- **Access:** official excerpt only, not the full book. Locators use printed pages; zero-based PDF indices are one less.
- **Passages:** pp. 9–11, five core need groupings and developmental possibilities; p. 16 and pp. 19–20, Other-Directedness and Approval-Seeking; pp. 22–23, conditional schemas; pp. 32–36, coping; Table 1.1, p. 39, schema-specific responses; pp. 37–44, modes; pp. 44–47, collaborative assessment and treatment components.
- **Use:** definition, needs framework, cautious origins, coping and therapy explanations.
- **Precision:** Table 1.1 assigns impressing others to surrender, avoiding people whose approval matters to avoidance, and provoking disapproval to overcompensation. Status-seeking or achievement is therefore not automatically overcompensation relative to this schema. Page 23 explicitly allows conditional schemas without an identified earlier schema.
- **Limits:** clinical conceptualization does not prove personal causes, memories, or a universal unmet-need profile. The historical footnote on p. 9 describes the then-current needs list as a clinical proposal; do not convert that into a claim that no subsequent research exists. The guide's cases are original applications.

## 2. International Society of Schema Therapy — definition and domain

<a id="ref-2"></a>
International Society of Schema Therapy. (n.d.). *Schema Therapy Central Concepts*. [Official overview](https://schematherapysociety.org/Schema-Therapy).

- **Access/locators:** full relevant webpage sections: Other-Directedness and schema 14; comparison definitions of Subjugation, Self-Sacrifice, Defectiveness/Shame, Enmeshment, Unrelenting Standards, and Entitlement.
- **Use:** the opening brief, domain link, and same-situation comparison of possible meanings. Approval and recognition can become the dominant basis for self-evaluation and choices, crowding out personal inclinations. Admiration-seeking differs conceptually from primarily seeking power or exemption.
- **Limits:** professional conceptual guidance, not a diagnostic test or observational finding. The guide uses Young/ISST's familiar five-domain map. Domains are not diagnoses or five separate established causes. Contextual counterexamples involving genuine evaluation requirements, culture, access, caregiving, or safety are original teaching applications.

## 3. Young and First — mode terminology

<a id="ref-3"></a>
Young, J. E., & First, M. (2003). *Schema Mode Listing*. Schema Therapy Institute. [Official listing](https://www.schematherapy.com/id72.htm).

- **Access/locators:** full listing, especially entries 1 and 5–10 and its 2003 author attribution. Opening the page at line 3 exposed the complete text when the initial extraction showed only the heading.
- **Use:** Vulnerable Child, Compliant Surrenderer, Detached Protector, Overcompensator, Punitive Parent, Demanding Parent, and Healthy Adult, using the project glossary. “Demanding Parent” is the guide's short form of “Demanding or Critical Parent.”
- **Precision:** interpersonal approval-seeking submission can fit Compliant Surrenderer; not every act of impressing is passive submission. Overcompensator requires a compensatory function. Demanding Parent concerns rigid requirements, whereas Punitive Parent concerns punitive enforcement/blame. A mode is temporary, not a fixed identity or a required part of every case.
- **Limits:** conceptual descriptions, not diagnostic criteria. Original cues and inner sentences illustrate possibilities and are nonspecific. Do not attribute later mode subtypes to this listing or treat emotional distance, disagreement, confidence, or ambition as sufficient evidence of a mode.

<a id="ref-4"></a>
Arntz, A., & Jacob, G. (2013). *Schema Therapy in Practice: An Introductory Guide to the Schema Mode Approach*. Wiley-Blackwell. [Official publisher excerpt](https://catalogimages.wiley.com/images/db/pdf/9781119962861.excerpt.pdf).

- **Passages:** p. 8, domain table; pp. 20–22, Other-Directedness and Approval-Seeking; pp. 26–28 and Table 1.2, needs and the clinical/heuristic nature of their mapping; pp. 29–33, coping; pp. 34–39, modes.
- **Use:** distinguishing favourable impressions and appreciation from one's own needs and wishes; function-based formulation; caution about simple domain-to-cause mappings.
- **Limits:** a clinical model is not an experimentally established personal history. Do not reproduce the excerpt's Sarah/lawyer vignette or make its developmental illustrations universal. References to imagery or chair work in the guide remain an overview of therapist-supported work, not unsupervised instructions or a claim to have consulted the full manual.

## 5. Saggino and colleagues — instrument-specific measurement

<a id="ref-5"></a>
Saggino, A., Balsamo, M., Carlucci, L., Cavalletti, V., Sergi, M. R., da Fermo, G., Dèttore, D., Marsigli, N., Petruccelli, I., Pizzo, S., & Tommasi, M. (2018). Psychometric properties of the Italian version of the Young Schema Questionnaire L-3: Preliminary results. *Frontiers in Psychology, 9*, 312. [DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00312](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00312). [Consulted full article](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00312/full).

- **Access/locators:** full article; Participants, Reliability and Convergent Validity, Tables 2 and 4–6, Discussion limitations.
- **Design/population:** measurement and concurrent self-report association study. Analysed 148 clinical and 918 community Italian adults after 46 exclusions; repeated-measurement participation declined to 40 at the final repeat.
- **Finding:** Approval-Seeking internal consistency was .916 in the clinical and .914 in the community group; repeat-measurement ICC was .766 (95% CI .643–.859), below the authors' .80 criterion. Structural fit was imperfect; the clinical sample's specific-domain Approval-Seeking loading was not significant in the tested hierarchical model.
- **Limits:** heterogeneous diagnoses, missing-data imputation, small retest sample, limited convergent constructs, one language/context, and no treatment-responsiveness test. Reliability, diagnostic validity, causal explanation, and treatment effectiveness are different questions. These findings do not validate the guide's original prompts or Persian translation. No questionnaire items, score thresholds, or false-positive classification rules are reproduced.

## 6. Cudo and colleagues — schema-specific observational association

<a id="ref-6"></a>
Cudo, A., Mącik, D., Griffiths, M. D., & Kuss, D. J. (2020). The relationship between problematic Facebook use and early maladaptive schemas. *Journal of Clinical Medicine, 9*(12), 3921. [DOI: 10.3390/jcm9123921](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm9123921). [Consulted full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7761769/).

- **Access/locators:** full article; Methods 2.1–2.3, Tables 2–3, final Discussion limitations.
- **Design/population:** cross-sectional self-report survey; 619 Polish Facebook users, including 568 women, ages 18–30. People who had played videogames during the preceding year were excluded.
- **Finding:** Approval-Seeking correlated with problematic Facebook use (Spearman rho .28) and remained positively associated in the adjusted model (standardized beta .25, p < .001).
- **Limits:** selected young, predominantly female, non-gaming sample; self-report, cultural and platform specificity; no causal direction or treatment test. Results do not make ordinary posting, feedback monitoring, or professional visibility a sign of a schema. Do not import the discussion's speculative parenting or narcissism pathways as findings.
- **Classification:** the paper uses a later four-domain grouping. Its association findings do not require adopting that grouping; the guide explicitly retains the Young/ISST five-domain teaching map. Questionnaire and Facebook-scale items are not reproduced.

## 7. Bamelis and colleagues — whole-treatment evidence

<a id="ref-7"></a>
Bamelis, L. L. M., Evers, S. M. A. A., Spinhoven, P., & Arntz, A. (2014). Results of a multicenter randomized controlled trial of the clinical effectiveness of schema therapy for personality disorders. *American Journal of Psychiatry, 171*(3), 305–322. [DOI: 10.1176/appi.ajp.2013.12040518](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.2013.12040518). [Publisher abstract and metadata](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/abs/10.1176/appi.ajp.2013.12040518).

- **Access:** abstract and bibliographic metadata only; the full article was not consulted. Publisher-indexed abstract text was available during source checking; direct publisher and PubMed extraction was intermittent.
- **Design/population:** single-blind randomized trial in 12 Dutch institutes; 323 adults with cluster C, paranoid, histrionic, or narcissistic personality disorders. Schema Therapy n = 147, usual care n = 135, clarification-oriented psychotherapy n = 41.
- **Finding:** 50-session Schema Therapy produced greater diagnostic recovery three years after treatment began than the comparison treatments. Interview-based results favoured Schema Therapy; self-report outcomes did not show between-treatment differences.
- **Limits:** full professional-treatment evidence in a specified clinical population, not an Approval-Seeking-specific effect, individual-technique effect, or self-help-guide trial. No diagnosis-specific estimates or unreported mechanisms are inferred from the abstract.

## Editorial decisions and excluded inferences

- Wanting connection, fair recognition, encouragement, or useful feedback is legitimate. The clinical question concerns rigidity, self-evaluation, costs, and the ability to make considered choices.
- Careful adaptation to workplace duties, disability-related support, cultural expectations, caregiving, discrimination, or unsafe circumstances may have practical reasons. Independent experiments are limited to manageable situations; essential review and safety requirements remain.
- Healthy change is neither universal approval nor forced indifference to others. Disagreement, disappointment, uncertainty, and meaningful feedback may remain.
- Surrender to Approval-Seeking can look socially active and successful. The same achievement may compensate for another fear; the guide should name that different formulation rather than assume it.
- Overcompensation examples may show reflexively opposing an admired person's opinion to disprove dependence on approval. Genuine dissent and healthy boundaries are counterexamples, not pathology.
- A 2023 Cudo follow-up was screened but not selected: its consulted publisher PDF contained inconsistent collection-date/sample-table information. The verified 2020 article supplies the selected narrow observational finding. Lindsey (2014) was screened at author-abstract level but not selected; its subgroup-specific regression results should not be flattened into a universal claim. Neither screened source is a numbered source supporting this guide.
- The preceding Entitlement guide's Pauwels study examines older 15/16-schema forms. It was not reused as measurement evidence for Approval-Seeking.
