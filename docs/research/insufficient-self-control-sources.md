# Insufficient Self-Control / Self-Discipline: authoring evidence record

Sources checked 15 October 2026. This is a claim-checking aid for the bilingual guide, not an additional assessment. The proposed wording below is deliberately narrower than some older clinical descriptions. Examples, recognition prompts, and worksheets in the guide are original teaching material, not research measures.

## 1. Young, Klosko, and Weishaar: conceptual foundation

Young, J. E., Klosko, J. S., & Weishaar, M. E. (2003). *Schema therapy: A practitioner's guide*. Guilford Press. [Official Chapter 1 excerpt](https://www.guilford.com/excerpts/young.pdf?t=1).

- **Read:** relevant portions of the accessible chapter, especially printed pp. 9–12, 15, 19, 32–39, and the treatment overview on pp. 44–47. The full book was not consulted.
- **Supports:** difficulty sustaining personally meaningful action through frustration or managing impulses; a less overt presentation emphasizing discomfort avoidance. The model places this schema in Impaired Limits. Its needs framework includes realistic limits alongside connection, autonomy, expression, and play.
- **Coping check:** Table 1.1, p. 39, distinguishes giving up on routine tasks, avoiding responsibility, and excessive self-discipline. Classify the function of a response, not its appearance.
- **Limits:** a clinical model, not evidence that a particular reader had a specified upbringing. The authors explicitly call their needs grouping a proposal on p. 9. Temperament and environment interact; no single family explanation is established.

## 2. International Society of Schema Therapy: concepts

International Society of Schema Therapy. (n.d.). *Schema therapy central concepts*. [Official webpage](https://schematherapysociety.org/Schema-Therapy).

- **Read:** schema definitions and domain descriptions, especially schema 11; distinctions relevant to Failure, Entitlement, Defectiveness, and Unrelenting Standards.
- **Supports:** the repeated conflict between immediate relief and longer-term goals, and costs of habitual discomfort avoidance.
- **Editorial use:** concise definition and distinctions among schemas. Use “may” for developmental accounts; this page's “typical family origin” wording cannot establish an individual's history.
- **Limits:** professional educational guidance, not a controlled study or diagnostic instrument.

## 3. Young and First: modes

Young, J. E., & First, M. (2003). *Schema mode listing*. Schema Therapy Institute. [Author-hosted listing](https://www.schematherapy.com/id72.htm).

- **Read:** complete listing; copyright year and authors verified at its end.
- **Supports:** Impulsive/Undisciplined Child as a mode involving immediate urges and difficulty waiting; Detached Protector as disconnection and distraction; Punitive Parent as harsh blame; Demanding Parent as rigid demands; Healthy Adult as supportive limits, responsibility, and enjoyment.
- **Editorial use:** paraphrase in respectful ordinary language. Explain that these are temporary states within a therapy model, not insults, diagnoses, or literal children inside the reader.
- **Limits:** conceptual taxonomy; it does not establish that every person has every named mode.

## 4. Bricker and Young: treatment overview

Bricker, D. C., & Young, J. E. (2004). *A client's guide to schema therapy*. Cognitive Therapy Center of New York. [Accessible author guide hosted by Schema Therapy Associates](https://schematherapyassociates.com/clients-guide-to-schema-therapy).

- **Read:** schema descriptions, coping styles, modes, and treatment overview; date verified from the copyright notice.
- **Supports:** examining evidence for beliefs, cognitive dialogue, behavioral rehearsal and changes, and therapist-supported experiential work.
- **Editorial use:** describe what a clinician may do with a person, rather than directing unsupported memory work. Explain collaboration and practical support.
- **Limits:** an educational account; its clinical examples are not treatment trial outcomes. Do not reproduce its case stories, flashcard, questionnaire items, or categorical claims about childhood causes.

## 5. Bamelis and colleagues: full-treatment trial

Bamelis, L. L. M., Evers, S. M. A. A., Spinhoven, P., & Arntz, A. (2014). Results of a multicenter randomized controlled trial of the clinical effectiveness of schema therapy for personality disorders. *American Journal of Psychiatry, 171*(3), 305–322. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.2013.12040518). [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24322378/).

- **Read:** abstract and bibliographic record, not the full article.
- **Design:** 323 patients at 12 Dutch services; 50-session schema therapy compared with clarification-oriented psychotherapy and usual treatment; several personality disorders, not a sample selected for this schema.
- **Supports:** greater interview-assessed diagnostic recovery in schema therapy three years after treatment began. Self-report measures did not show the same between-treatment differences.
- **Limits:** evidence concerns a comprehensive clinician-delivered treatment. It does not establish effectiveness for this schema alone, an individual exercise, or this website. Do not describe it as a self-control-specific trial.

## 6. Aftab and colleagues: observational schema study

Aftab, S., Klibert, J., Holtzman, N., Qadeer, K., & Aftab, S. (2017). Schemas mediate the link between procrastination and depression: Results from the United States and Pakistan. *Journal of Rational-Emotive & Cognitive-Behavior Therapy, 35*(4), 329–345. [DOI and publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10942-017-0263-5). [Author university record](https://scholars.georgiasouthern.edu/en/publications/schemas-mediate-the-link-between-procrastination-and-depression-r-2/).

- **Read:** publisher and university abstracts; full text is subscription content and was not consulted.
- **Design:** self-report questionnaires from 412 US and 240 Pakistani college students; statistical mediation models involving procrastination, depressive symptoms, Defectiveness, and Insufficient Self-Control.
- **Supports:** an indirect association through Insufficient Self-Control was significant in the US sample, but not in the Pakistani sample. The test of moderation by nationality was not significant.
- **Limits:** this is an association, not demonstration that the schema causes depression or procrastination. Do not infer a proven cultural difference from one significant and one nonsignificant path. College samples and self-report limit generalization. No treatment was tested.

## 7. Saulsman and Nathan: supplementary CBT guidance

Saulsman, L., & Nathan, P. (2008). *Put off procrastinating*. Centre for Clinical Interventions, Perth, Western Australia. [Official resource page](https://cci.health.wa.gov.au/Resources/Looking-After-Yourself/Procrastination).

- **Read:** resource page and relevant content in [Module 5: Practical techniques to stop procrastination](https://cci.health.wa.gov.au/~/media/CCI/Consumer-Modules/Put-Off-Procrastinating/Putting-Off-Procrastinating---05---Practical-Techniques-to-stop-Procrastination.pdf) and [Module 6: Adjusting unhelpful rules/assumptions and tolerating discomfort](https://cci.health.wa.gov.au/~/media/CCI/Consumer-Modules/Put-Off-Procrastinating/Putting-Off-Procrastinating---06---Adjusting-Unhelpful-Rules-Assumptions-and-Tolerating-Discomfort.pdf). Authorship/year verified in each module's final page.
- **Module 5 supports:** splitting tasks into steps, realistic timing, short starts, explicit stopping times, arranging a helpful environment, and seeking support.
- **Module 6 supports:** reviewing rigid assumptions and observing tolerable discomfort without immediately escaping it.
- **Limits:** supplementary CBT skills; not a trial of schema therapy or this guide. Do not equate all delay with this schema. Do not generalize the workbook's fatigue discussion into advice to override illness, exhaustion, pain, or needed rest. The guide's low-consequence experiment is an original exercise, not a reproduced CCI worksheet.

## 8. NIMH: broader assessment

National Institute of Mental Health. (2024). *Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder: What you need to know* (NIH Publication No. 24-MH-8300; revised 2024). U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, National Institutes of Health. [Official publication](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-what-you-need-to-know).

- **Read:** diagnosis, adult presentation, and psychosocial support sections; publication details verified at the end.
- **Supports:** attention and impulse difficulties merit assessment of development, functioning across settings, medical and mental health history, and alternative explanations. Stress, sleep disorders, anxiety, depression, and physical illness can produce overlapping symptoms.
- **Editorial use:** a schema label does not establish or rule out ADHD. Persistent functional difficulty can warrant a clinician's assessment and appropriate supports. No diagnostic symptom counts need be reproduced in this guide.
- **Limits:** general clinical education, not research demonstrating equivalence between ADHD and any schema.

Young, J. E., & Klosko, J. S. (1994). *Reinventing your life: The breakthrough program to end negative behavior and feel great again*. Plume. ISBN 9780452272040. [Publisher's edition](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/327709/reinventing-your-life-by-jeffrey-e-young/).

- **Verified:** publisher metadata identifies the Plume edition as 1 May 1994, 384 pages.
- **Scope:** general further reading about schema-based self-help. The relevant book content was not consulted; do not cite it as evidence for particular claims or imply it is a dedicated treatment manual for this schema.

## Cross-cutting editorial decisions

- Distinguish choosing rest, making a realistic limit, lacking resources, and involuntary executive-function difficulty from a repetitive interpretation-and-response pattern.
- Avoid “lazy,” “spoiled,” “weak-willed,” or moral worth as explanatory labels. Historical labels may be named briefly, with an ordinary-language explanation.
- Support accountability without punishment: a missed promise can require repair while the person remains worthy of respect.
- The maintenance sequence and example alternatives are teaching formulations, not established causal pathways for every reader.
- The available evidence does not validate the guide's prompts, assign diagnostic thresholds, or prove the page changes clinical outcomes.
