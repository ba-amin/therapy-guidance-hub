# Enmeshment / Undeveloped Self: authoring evidence record

Sources reviewed **16 October 2026**. This record supports the bilingual guide and its [authoring outline](../drafts/enmeshment-undeveloped-self.md). Reference numbers are fixed across those documents and the HTML guide. It records what was actually consulted; access to an excerpt does not imply review of the full book.

The guide's cases, dialogue, recognition prompts, cycle, mode cues, and worksheets are original teaching illustrations. They are not questionnaire items, diagnostic criteria, observed prevalence, or tested treatment protocols. No numerical self-assessment or severity threshold is justified by these sources.

## 1. Young, Klosko, and Weishaar — conceptual foundation

Young, J. E., Klosko, J. S., & Weishaar, M. E. (2003). *Schema therapy: A practitioner's guide*. Guilford Press. [Official Chapter 1 excerpt](https://www.guilford.com/excerpts/young.pdf?t=1).

- **Material consulted:** definition on printed pp. 15 and 18; needs and developmental discussion, pp. 9–12; coping and modes, pp. 32–44; treatment overview, pp. 44–47. The PDF's zero-based page index is one below the printed page number.
- **Claim supported:** this schema concerns relational involvement that constrains identity and autonomy; the model places it in Impaired Autonomy and Performance.
- **Coping locator:** Table 1.1, printed p. 38, includes this schema. Read columns together; do not infer a coping style from an action alone.
- **Needs locator:** pp. 9–10 present a proposed five-need framework, including attachment and autonomy/identity. The authors acknowledge the framework's theoretical status.
- **Use:** concise model explanation, followed by original examples. The Sara story is not adapted from a published case.
- **Limit:** conceptual and clinical material, not proof of any reader's family history, the accuracy of remembered events, or this guide's effectiveness. Do not import the chapter's older efficacy assertions as current empirical findings.

## 2. Young and First — mode terminology

Young, J. E., & First, M. (2003). *Schema mode listing*. Schema Therapy Institute. [Author-hosted listing](https://www.schematherapy.com/id72.htm).

- **Material consulted:** complete listing and end-of-page copyright attribution.
- **Locators:** entries 1, 5–10: Vulnerable Child; Compliant Surrenderer; Detached Protector; Overcompensator; Punitive Parent; Demanding or Critical Parent; Healthy Adult.
- **Claim supported:** modes describe active states within the model. Demanding Parent imposes rules; Punitive Parent condemns or punishes. Healthy Adult combines care, practical responsibility, and proportionate limits.
- **Editorial use:** use the hub's glossary. The shorter heading “Demanding Parent” is an editorial label for the listing's “Demanding or Critical Parent.” Avoid adding later subtypes without another source.
- **Limit:** taxonomy, not a diagnostic checklist or schema-to-mode lookup. Body sensations and dialogue supplied for Sara are nonspecific, original illustrations. “Child” and “Parent” do not establish literal parts, immaturity, or a particular parent's intentions.

## 3. International Society of Schema Therapy — schema definitions

International Society of Schema Therapy. (n.d.). *Schema therapy central concepts*. [Official webpage](https://schematherapysociety.org/Schema-Therapy).

- **Material consulted:** schema 8 and its domain; adjacent definitions for Dependence/Incompetence, Subjugation, Self-Sacrifice, Abandonment/Instability, and Approval-Seeking/Recognition-Seeking.
- **Claim supported:** relational fusion and an insufficiently distinct identity are central concerns; everyday dependence, fear of abandonment, fearful submission, self-sacrifice, and approval-seeking have different emphases.
- **Use:** brief definition and comparison of the meaning attached to the same event. Preserve overlap and uncertainty.
- **Limit:** professional educational material. Its descriptions of typical origins do not establish an individual's cause. Shared interests, family consultation, caregiving, cultural belonging, and needed assistance cannot be classified from appearance alone.

## 4. Arntz and Jacob — clinical elaboration and treatment context

Arntz, A., & Jacob, G. (2013). *Schema therapy in practice: An introductory guide to the schema mode approach* (1st ed.). Wiley-Blackwell. [Official publisher excerpt](https://catalogimages.wiley.com/images/db/pdf/9781119962861.excerpt.pdf).

- **Material consulted:** chapter “Basics”; PDF pages 3–4 (zero-based indices 2–3) on treatment emphases and printed pp. 16–17 on Enmeshment/Undeveloped Self. Printed p. 16 is PDF page 15 (zero-based index 14).
- **Claim supported:** practical achievement can coexist with difficulty recognizing personal feelings, opinions, and preferences. A relationship may feel rewarding while autonomy elsewhere remains constrained.
- **Treatment locator:** opening chapter discusses experiential work, imagery, chair dialogue, and the therapeutic relationship. These are descriptions of clinician-delivered approaches.
- **Use:** accessible distinction between practical competence and a clearer personal identity; brief explanation of supported therapy work.
- **Limit:** no schema-specific effect estimate or proof that an exercise works. Do not reproduce the book's case or describe the full book as consulted. Do not infer obsessive-compulsive disorder from this schema.

## 5. Baroncelli and colleagues — direct observational evidence

Baroncelli, C. M. C., Lodder, P., van der Lee, M., & Bachrach, N. (2025). The role of enmeshment and undeveloped self, subjugation and self-sacrifice in childhood trauma and attachment related problems: The relationship with self-concept clarity. *Acta Psychologica, 254*, Article 104839. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2025.104839). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40020289/). [University record and published-version link](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/the-role-of-enmeshment-and-undeveloped-self-subjugation-and-self-/).

- **Material consulted:** abstract and full published article via the [authors' public upload](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389390171_The_role_of_enmeshment_and_undeveloped_self_subjugation_and_self-sacrifice_in_childhood_trauma_and_attachment_related_problems_The_relationship_with_self-concept_clarity), especially Methods §2.1–2.2, Results §3, and Limitations §4.4.
- **Design/population:** cross-sectional self-report study of 360 psychology students; approximately 84% female and predominantly white. Path models examined three schemas, recalled childhood trauma, attachment, and self-concept clarity.
- **Essential result:** **Enmeshment's combined pathways to self-concept clarity were not statistically significant in the final model**; opposing anxiety/avoidance paths canceled. Do not substitute the abstract's broad conclusion for this result.
- **Use:** show that direct research exists but is mixed and limited.
- **Limits:** no causal inference, treatment test, or demonstrated universal sequence. The introduction's distinction between the schema's two aspects is clinical theory attributed to Young, not a factor separation demonstrated here.

## 6. Yalcin and colleagues — measurement caution

Yalcin, O., Marais, I., Lee, C., & Correia, H. M. (2022). Revisions to the Young Schema Questionnaire using Rasch analysis: The YSQ-R. *Australian Psychologist, 57*(1), 8–20. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/00050067.2021.1979885). [University abstract and bibliographic record](https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/en/publications/revisions-to-the-young-schema-questionnaire-using-rasch-analysis-/).

- **Material consulted:** publisher abstract and university abstract/metadata only; full article not reviewed. Online publication was 2021; the journal issue is dated **2022**.
- **Design/population:** Rasch analysis of 838 participants, including 574 clinical and 264 nonclinical participants; refinement of the YSQ-L3 into the YSQ-R.
- **Essential result:** the revised Enmeshment subscale's reported reliability was **.57**, compared with .74–.86 for the other refined subscales.
- **Use:** assessment precision depends on version and sample; a number does not settle an individual's formulation.
- **Limits:** instrument-specific result. Do not call .57 Cronbach's alpha, generalize it to all YSQ forms or translations, treat it as a clinical cutoff, or claim that it disproves the schema. No copyrighted assessment items are reproduced.

## 7. Arntz and colleagues — whole-treatment randomized trial

Arntz, A., Jacob, G. A., Lee, C. W., Brand-de Wilde, O. M., Fassbinder, E., Harper, R. P., Lavender, A., Lockwood, G., Malogiannis, I. A., Ruths, F. A., Schweiger, U., Shaw, I. A., Zarbock, G., & Farrell, J. M. (2022). Effectiveness of predominantly group schema therapy and combined individual and group schema therapy for borderline personality disorder: A randomized clinical trial. *JAMA Psychiatry, 79*(4), 287–299. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2022.0010). [Full publisher article](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/2789694).

- **Material consulted:** full article, including abstract, intervention description, primary outcomes, and limitations; author list verified against publisher metadata.
- **Design/population:** 495 adults with borderline personality disorder randomized across 15 sites in five countries; 494 analyzed after one consent withdrawal. Two years of treatment, assessments through three years.
- **Comparison/outcome:** combined individual and group schema therapy improved BPD severity more than optimal usual treatment and predominantly group schema therapy. Predominantly group schema therapy did **not** significantly outperform usual treatment on the primary outcome.
- **Limit:** evidence for a complete, professionally delivered treatment in that population. Not a trial selected for Enmeshment/Undeveloped Self, a self-help guide, or an isolated identity/boundary exercise.

## 8. Michel / Centre for Clinical Interventions — supplementary practical guidance

Michel, F. (2008). *Assert yourself!* Centre for Clinical Interventions, Perth, Western Australia. ISBN 0-9757995-5-X. [Official resource page](https://cci.health.wa.gov.au/Resources/Looking-After-Yourself/Assertiveness).

- **Material consulted:** [Module 1: What is assertiveness?](https://cci.health.wa.gov.au/~/media/CCI/Consumer-Modules/Assert-Yourself/Assert-Yourself---01---What-is-Assertiveness.pdf), especially pp. 2–3, 6, and authorship p. 9; [Module 6: How to say “no” assertively](https://cci.health.wa.gov.au/~/media/CCI/Consumer-Modules/Assert-Yourself/Assert-Yourself---06---How-to-Say-No-Assertively.pdf), especially pp. 3–5 and authorship p. 8.
- **Claim supported:** express a preference or refusal clearly while respecting others; consider context; communication cannot guarantee agreement or a desired outcome.
- **Use:** supplementary CBT-informed communication guidance. The webpage's 2019 update is not the workbook publication date.
- **Limits:** no evidence here for this schema's treatment or the guide's original planner. Do not reproduce the workbook's inventory or scoring. Do not generalize its categorical workplace example into a rule that employees must always comply.

## Editorial checks before publication

- Keep evidence types separate: conceptual framework [1–4], observational associations [5], psychometric research [6], whole-treatment trial [7], and supplementary skills resource [8].
- Explain enmeshment and undeveloped-self as related aspects, without implying that one behavior proves either or that a person must fit every feature.
- Treat culture, household structure, disability-related help, financial constraints, caregiving, and actual relationship safety as context. Autonomy concerns meaningful choice; it does not require living alone, rejecting help, or ending close relationships.
- Keep possible origins tentative; no memory-recovery task, assumed trauma, or automatic blame of relatives.
- For boundary experiments, use modest choices in sufficiently safe situations. When disagreement may provoke harm, consider support and safety rather than assigning exposure to the conflict.
- Retain exactly eight reflection fields and six experiment fields, shared across languages. State that prompts are unscored and original; do not store or submit responses.
- Keep references as the last clinical content section and match citations and qualifications in English and Persian.
