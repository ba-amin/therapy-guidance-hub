# Reusable schema guide template draft

Copy this outline when drafting a guide. Follow the [authoring standard](schema-guide-standard.md) for the common structure, bilingual text, exercises, evidence, and styles. Replace bracketed prompts before turning the draft into reader-facing content.

Record the English and Persian schema names, intended reader, source edition, planned filename, and review status in the authoring notes. In the finished page, show a clear title, a brief description, and an accurate content review date. Reading-time estimates should reflect the finished text.

**Minimum introduction and recognition coverage:** use the [Defectiveness / Shame guide](../Interactive%20Guide%20to%20the%20Defectiveness%20Shame%20Schema.html) as a content-depth benchmark and complete the standard's [coverage checklist](schema-guide-standard.md#coverage-checklist-before-publication). Every guide needs a brief definition/domain/needs summary, a dedicated core-emotional-needs discussion with examples, and separate visible and hidden/masked signs. Preserve fifteen sections; section 3 now explicitly names core emotional needs. Retain this template's evidence, glossary, and references-last conventions when adapting the older guide.

**Required for every schema:** complete all parts of section 7. Carry its coping functions, mode activation cues, and repeated sequence into the cycle, examples, worksheets, practice, and progress review. Follow the standard's [coverage requirements and glossary](schema-guide-standard.md#required-coverage-of-coping-styles-and-modes). Use existing shared styles and interactions for the finished HTML.

## 11 Practicing change

[Offer a small choice the reader can adapt. Explain the purpose, appropriate context, and what to observe. Include a completed fictional experiment using the six fields: situation and limits, prediction, small action, support or stopping conditions, observed outcome, and learning.]

Sample learning prompt: “What did I observe, what remains uncertain, and what would I adjust next time?” Label the exercise as original and avoid promising that a different response will produce agreement, acceptance, or immediate relief.
